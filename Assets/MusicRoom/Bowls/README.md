# Singing bowls (`MusicRoom.Bowls`, id `bowls`)

Three singing bowls on ring cushions on a small walnut stand left of the player's knees: **big G3** (nearest the left
shoulder), **middle C4**, **small E4**. Hands only: tap with a fingertip, strike with the palm, circle a fingertip round
the outside of the rim (or on top of it) until the bowl sings, rest a palm on a bowl to quiet it, or pinch the puja (the
bowl stick) to strike with its suede end and rub with its wooden shaft. Spec: `Docs/12-new-instruments/bowls.md`
(revision 2) and the review items (`PHASE-B-NOTES.md` section 3). Lab: the bowls reference (Node). Platform ids: Kind / Rig /
Unit 9, `SourceId.Bowls` (25), `BowlsModule` Order 37, TickOrder 24, carousel 3.

## Events (`BowlsEvents`)

`bowlStrike` Onset (Index), `bowlRubOn` Onset / `bowlRubCtl` Control / `bowlRubOff` Release (Lane), `bowlDamp` State
(Index, sampled), `bowlSing` and `bowlHit` Markers (Index). Fields: `i` bowl; strikes `vel` speed m/s, `kind` tap |
palm | suede | wood, `midi` the angle (mrad), `pos` 2 (1 - s); rubs `zone`, `u` finger speed, `vel` level = N / 8,
`pos`, `midi`, `speed` (glide), live `lane` = the bowl's index; damp `vel` = level / 3; `bowlSing.u` seconds sung. The
tutorial counts an instrument's Markers when it declares any, so a live strike also posts `bowlHit` (in a lesson, not
while the event waits for a rub on that bowl: tap, then circle is no mistake) and singing posts `bowlSing` once it
reaches the rub target's hold.

## Live against playback (review item 14)

One `LaneArbiter` per bowl: the live hand (lane 0) takes a bowl from a loop or a take (the unit re-seats the stick from
the hand), a playback lane never takes it from the hand, the loop's Release while the hand rubs is ignored, and the
loop's next control takes the bowl back once the hand lifts. A level-0 control never wakes a free bowl.

## Controls (what the tips say, and what the code does)

| Gesture | Rule |
|---|---|
| Tap | a fingertip entering a bowl at >= 0.12 m/s inward; a slow poke rings |
| Rub | a touching fingertip moving along the rim (>= 0.05 m/s for 0.1 s), outside wall or on the lip (both sing); the inside never sings |
| Faster / slower | sings from about 0.1 to 0.6 m/s; "about one slow circle a second" is 0.4-0.55 m/s; light and fast chatters |
| Damping | one still fingertip after 0.6 s: a slow fade; two fingers 0.8 s; a resting palm 0.28 s |
| The stick | pinch within 5 cm of it (Meta's pinch counts); suede end strikes, the shaft strikes and rubs; open the hand 0.25 s to set it down; a holding hand's fingertips do not play |
| Desktop | click a bowl: a tap (0.8 m/s at the clicked point); keep the button down and circle: a rub at 4 N; release: lift |

## A rub that does not crackle (rub smoothing)

On the headset the tracker gives a new fingertip sample every 20-40 ms (Fast Motion Mode off: 20 ms, on: 40 ms), with
2-3 mm of jitter and now and then a jump of a few centimetres. `BowlsHands` derives each frame's rub from it: the force
from the depth (250 N/m), the speed along the rim from the angle's rate, the contact angle and height. Sent raw, the
force moved in steps (the stick, a mass on a spring against the outer wall, bounced off and slapped back), a jump made one
huge speed (the friction jerked) and the angle's jitter moved the contact along the wall in steps: the "broken cassette"
crackle while rubbing (striking was fine). With `RubSmoothing` (on; off = the reference's raw values) the angular-rate
estimate clamps a jump (`OMEGA_JUMP` from the running value, `VF_MAX` along the rim), and every rub frame sent is
conditioned: `|v_f| <= VF_MAX` (1.2 m/s) slewed by `VF_SLEW` (3 m/s^2), the force a one-pole (`N_TAU` 50 ms) slewed by
`N_SLEW` (40 N/s), the angle carried on by the speed and pulled toward the measured one (`A_TAU` 50 ms). The unit already
ramps each frame's force and speed per sample (512 samples, 30 ms in and out at a touch's start and end), and the rub
encoder's playback ramps (`Rate` 48000) match the unit, which only runs at 48 kHz. Classification (rub start, the rub
mean) reads the raw speed. `BowlsRubClickTests` rubs the big bowl smooth (90 Hz tracker) and jittery (25 Hz, 3 mm, one
4 cm jump): the worst 1 ms window of the output's second difference over the median window is 3.3 smooth, 40 jittery raw
and 5.5 jittery with the smoothing (the limit: 3 x the smooth rub). The unit's cost is far from the budget (BowlsPerfTests:
two rubbed bowls about 20 us per 256-frame block in the editor, against the 1.5 ms block budget).

## Grazing the rim and the stick in the hand (smooth contact, the pad, the smooth stick)

The second headset report: crackle worst with the finger or stick on the outer side or just outside the bowl, and the
stick hard to circle. Reproduced in `BowlsGrazeTests` (60 Hz tracker, 72 Hz frames, 3 mm jitter, the puja posed through
`BowlsPuja` from a jittery wrist and pinch every frame). The cause: a rub that left the wall past the sticky band sat in
the lift hold at N_MIN against a loudly singing bowl, and the unit's stick (a 20 g mass on a 1e5 N/m spring) bounced off
the vibrating wall: the friction switched on and off and every re-impact was a click (10 % of the contact samples
"lost"). Fixes:

- The unit's soft pad (`BowlsDesign.SetPad`, on in `BowlsUnit`, off for the reference vectors): on an outer contact the
  bowl feels the stick's force blended 30 % toward N and low-passed at 1.2 kHz, and below 5 N the stick is pressed at 5 N
  with the forces scaled by N / 5 N (the mean force is unchanged), so a light touch, a swell-in or a release never lets it
  bounce. The chatter statistic (Sunny's "slower") still reads the stick's own bounces.
- `BowlsHands.SmoothContact`: a contact swells in from zero force; the force eases off from 8 mm outside the wall to 60 %
  at the sticky band's edge and fades out (50 ms) in the lift hold instead of holding N_MIN; the height (hs) is smoothed
  (80 ms) like the angle; a touching tip leaves only 6 mm out (was 3 mm, the jitter); a re-entry within 0.3 s that never
  went 6 mm away is the same touch (no tap, no tick); the puja's shaft point is the continuous deepest point (a ternary
  search, not 11 samples 11 mm apart), held on the bowl and side it rubs, kept in place when nothing touches; the stick's speed (strike detection) is smoothed over 20 ms.
- `BowlsPuja.Smooth`: the grip and the axis through a One Euro filter (1.5 Hz still, +25 Hz per m/s, a 6 Hz speed estimate; a 1.5 m/s strike lags at most 9.5 mm), starting in the hand
  at pick-up; while the shaft rubs, the drawn stick eases onto the rim (`SetRim`, 60 ms, at most 3 cm; the hand logic still
  reads the filtered hand's ends, so the depth sets the force).

Measured (TUNE-BOWLS.md in the work copy has the full table): a finger leaving to 22 mm outside, old 25.3 / 12 click
windows, new 7.0 / 0; the stick, old 14.7 / 4 with force steps of 3.2 N, new 6.2 / 0 with 0.56 N; the drawn stick's end
shake in a jittery hand 4.92 -> 1.85 mm per frame^2 (a jitter-free hand: 0.23), its turn 72 -> 28 mrad per frame^2, 1.2 mm
from the jitter-free stick, the drawn shaft 2.0 mm from the bronze while it rubs.

## A rub that sings instead of hissing (the tonal rub noise)

The third headset report: still "broken cassette" on every rub. A recording of the app's output (listen/nov9) showed a
smooth broadband noise, flat 0.9 to 3.5 kHz with the shape of the unit's own rub-noise filter, no peaks at the partials,
only 3 to 18 dB under the 196 Hz fundamental per 10 Hz bin, while the DSP load was at most 6 %. Not chatter, not CPU: the
reference's rub noise follows the friction power (F_c x slip speed) alone, and a rub slips at full power from its first
moment while the sing takes seconds to build (longer when fast, or with the puja), so most of a real rub was hiss.
`BowlsRubNoiseTests` reproduces it (60 Hz tracker, 3 to 4 mm jitter, 72 and 90 Hz frames, 0.1 to 0.9 m/s, the pressure
breathing, a finger and the puja in a jittery hand). The fix is in the unit (`BowlsDesign.SetRubNoise`, see
`Audio/Sound/Bowls/README.md`): the noise is capped against the bowl's own tone. Development players log one line a second
while a bowl is rubbed (`BowlsRubLog`: `[Bowls] rub bowl .. speed .. N .. Fc .. stick .. slip-frac .. lost .. level ..`).
