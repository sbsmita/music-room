# VR Music Room (Unity, Meta Quest 3 / 3S)

A cozy living room at night that you play with your bare hands: a full 88-key piano with a music book on its rest, a
drum kit with two sticks you can pick up, a 22-string harp, a handpan, a pair of hand drums, a marimba and
three singing bowls. Up to three sit with you at a time; the others stand around the room until you bring one. A fire crackles, snow falls past the window, and Sunny, a small sun-faced robot, shows
you around the first time you start the app. At launch a start panel asks where to play: "Play here" (the music room)
or "Play in my room" (mixed reality with passthrough: the piano lands on your real table, snow falls behind your real
windows). A palm menu holds lessons (36 guided lessons with falling blocks), the instrument layout, "Move instruments"
(place every instrument where it is comfortable for you; the app remembers it), your recordings (a tape deck on the
side table records and plays back what you play, and loops what you play in up to six layers), "Play in my room", the
tour and Exit.

A native Unity app for Meta Quest 3 and 3S. Every model, texture and sound is generated by the app's own code (no
recordings, no downloaded assets, no network access). Hands only: no controllers needed.

- Unity 6000.3.27f1 (Unity 6.3), URP 17.3, OpenXR with Meta's feature group, Meta XR Core SDK 207, MRUK 207, Meta XR
  Audio 85, Burst 1.8.31 + Mathematics 1.3.3
- Package `com.vrmusicroom.app`, version 0.1.0, Quest 3 and Quest 3S (Horizon OS v60 or later), 90 Hz with a quality
  governor that falls back to 72 Hz when the headset cannot keep up
- Built APK: `Builds/Android/VRMusicRoom.apk` (75,768,428 bytes, about 72.3 MiB)

## All the instruments (2026-11-01)

The five placeholders are now real instruments, and the piano has a music book. Each was built on its own on the
frozen many-instrument platform and then merged onto the first five minutes. The record is sections 20.14 and 21 of
`Docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`; each instrument's `README.md`, `PHASE-B-NOTES.md` and `CONTRACT-REQUESTS.md` are in its
folder.

- **Handpan**: 9 notes (D3 "Celtic minor", all white keys) on a low walnut stand in front of your knees; on display it
  hangs on a bracket on the left wall. Calm voice.
- **Hand drums**: a conga and a tumba on basket stands: open tone, bass, slap, mute, heel-toe, rolls; on display by the
  far end of the window. The drawn palm rides on the skin instead of sinking into it.
- **Marimba**: 37 wooden bars, C3 to C6, played with two yarn mallets or your fingertips; on display under the window.
  It never shares a set with the drum kit (the hi-hat would sit under your right hand).
- **Singing bowls**: three bowls (G3, C4, E4) left of your knees: tap, strike with the palm, circle a fingertip round the
  rim until it sings, or use the bowl stick; on display on the window sill.
- **The music book** on the piano's rest prints the piano lessons as sheet music with the key letter under every note,
  turns itself to the running lesson and marks the next note; tap a page's bottom corner to turn, a song's name to start
  it, the red ribbon to go back to the list.
- Every instrument has 3 lessons, records and loops on the deck, and has its own Sunny tips (each line names the real
  control); Sunny's coverage test counts every instrument's and the book's tips.
- **Removed (2026-11-10, at the player's request): the theremin.** A save that still names it opens the piano instead
  (One alone) or leaves it out of My set, its own settings are deleted on boot (`Core/Registry/RetiredInstruments.cs`),
  and an old recording plays everything else and skips its notes.

## The many-instrument platform, phase 0 (2026-11-01)

## What the first five minutes added (2026-11-01, merged onto the platform)

For first-time players (the judges of the First Five Minutes award), music comes first and the menu last. Record and
merge notes: `Assets/MusicRoom/Guide/FIRSTFIVE-NOTES.md`.

- **The first run.** Under the start panel Sunny waits; at 8 s it comes beside the panel and shows how to poke "Play here"
  (it never chooses for you). Then, with no countdown, a pale block falls onto a key: "Hi, I'm Sunny! Touch the glowing
  key." and a pale ghost hand presses it; the answering phrase of Ode to Joy follows block by block with soft chords under
  it (after 15 s without a right note, any key plays the next one). When the song ends the room answers: the fire flares,
  the lamps glow warmer, snow starts past the window and the harp rings a chord. Then a drum on the right, a nod for the
  kick, a sweep of the harp, the palm menu and what is in it (the rows light up as Sunny names them), and goodbye, with an
  invitation to the other instruments (the ghost hand points at one across the room and pinches; "You can also bring
  them from Instruments in the menu."): about 1-3 minutes. Every step shows itself again after 8 s and moves on after
  about 15 s; **poke Sunny to skip** any step.
- **Sunny's tour covers everything.** 65 tips of the tour, each the first time it matters, each with a ghost hand at the
  real control (a stick on the snare, the hi-hat stand, every deck button, the Done button, a menu row...), never over
  your playing; doing it yourself counts. They share ONE scheduler with the platform's six and every instrument's own
  tips, so Sunny never says two at once and the same quiet moments hold for all. Menu > Sunny: Show me around, Tips
  (every tip, hear any again), Make a song in layers (the deck's GUIDE), Tips while I play.
- **Lessons:** 36 lessons with corrected melodies (all public domain), easiest first; drum targets sit on the side of each
  drum facing you (no longer hidden inside a cymbal or on its bell); hitting a note twice is no mistake; after a lesson
  "Next: <title>"; finished lessons are remembered; the blocks and the ghost hand are pale and warm instead of glowing cyan
  (the ghost hand is a soft, rounded pale hand whose edges fade).
- **Looper:** the first note of a loop lands exactly on beat 1. **Move instruments:** the Done / Reset buttons stop your
  finger on their face like the menu.

## What the merge of "Move instruments" and the looper added (2026-11-01)

After the user's first headset sessions (no way into mixed reality without a gesture, no Exit, instruments that could
not be dragged) and the request for a looper, two feature clones were merged into the upgraded project (records:
`Assets/MusicRoom/Layout/ARRANGE-NOTES.md`, `Assets/MusicRoom/Lessons/LOOPER-NOTES.md`, and section 21 of
`Docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`):

- **A start panel** at launch: "Play here" or "Play in my room", so mixed reality needs no gesture. Sunny's first tour
  waits for the answer.
- **Exit** as the menu's last row, with "Tap again to exit".
- **Move instruments** (menu): the instruments go quiet and are marked by soft warm-white corner brackets; pinch one
  (or point at it with the ray from your hand and pinch) and carry it, raise or lower the piano and the drums for your
  seat, turn it; "Done" and "Reset" float close by. Your placement is remembered for each layout. Sunny says "Pinch an
  instrument to pick it up." the first time.
- **A steadier pinch everywhere.** The headset's own pinch detection now counts beside the fingertip distance, for
  moving instruments, picking up a stick, the hi-hat stand, turning the seat, bringing everything back and placing the
  piano in your room. The two fingertip joints sit about 17 mm apart when your fingertips touch, above the 15 mm the
  distance rule needed, so pinches used to flicker on the headset. The palm-toward-your-face system pinch still does
  nothing in the app.
- **A looper on the tape deck**: LOOP, a count-in, 2 or 4 bars at 70 / 90 / 110 BPM, up to six layers with mute, UNDO
  and CLEAR, a metronome, a timing tidy, sessions saved to Recordings. Every loop note is scheduled on the audio clock
  to the sample, on every instrument, and a loop never drifts.
- **GUIDE** on the deck: Sunny walks you through recording, playing back and building a loop in layers.

## What the upgrade changed (2026-11-01)

The room now sounds and behaves like a real one. Specs: `Docs/10-upgrade/`.

- **A new sound engine.** All new sounds run as Burst-compiled code on the audio thread, inside one call per audio block
  (`Assets/MusicRoom/Audio/Burst`: SIMD modal banks, waveguides and filters, sample-accurate events, voice stealing, a CPU
  cap with level-of-detail, denormal protection). Nothing on the audio thread allocates memory.
- **A physically modelled piano** (`Audio/Sound/Piano`): coupled unison strings struck by felt hammers, a soundboard, the
  sympathetic and duplex resonance of the other strings, real dampers, and a half pedal. The automatic sustain now uses
  the half pedal and damps released neighbours, so scales stay clear. It is computed live, so nothing is rendered at
  start-up.
- **A modelled drum kit** (`Audio/Sound/Drums`): membranes whose pitch glides on hard hits, snare wires that buzz when the
  kick sounds, cymbals with real wash, hand chokes, an opening hi-hat, two sticks on one drum, a cross-stick on the snare
  rim, and slightly different strokes every time.
- **A physical harp** (`Audio/Sound/Harp`): 22 strings on one shared soundboard. A string catches when you pluck it again,
  rings in sympathy with its neighbours, and stops under a finger or palm. The strings now blur on screen the way the
  sound decays.
- **Our own room acoustics** (`Audio/Sound/Room`): early reflections from the room's real walls, window and curtains for
  each instrument, heard from where your head is; a late reverb tail tuned to the room; and a new master stage with a
  true-peak limiter. In "Play in my room" it models your real room. (New curtains hang by the window.)
- **Back to the original sound (2026-11-10).** After the headset test the original sounds won: the piano, the drums,
  the harp and the room always play them (today's synths and Meta's room acoustics). The new piano, drum, harp and room
  units above are no longer created, so they cost no CPU or memory, and the menu has no sound switch.
- **The upgraded sounds deleted (2026-11-11).** "I want only classic sound": the four units above (`Audio/Sound/Piano`,
  `Drums`, `Harp`, `Room`, the drum kit's 276 KB data file included), their lab folders (`Tools/SoundLab/piano`, `harp`),
  the room's listener stage and source taps, the physical harp's and modelled kit's paths in the instruments, and the
  tests that only exercised them are gone (backup: `music-room-vr-wip/unity-backups/pre-classic-cleanup/`). The
  Burst graph stays for the four unit-only instruments.
- **Hands and world:** fingers stop on the keys, drum heads and menu instead of sinking in; sticks bounce off the heads;
  soft contact shadows under fingers and sticks; lit skin for your hands in VR and a clean cut-out in mixed reality;
  cymbals rock and drum heads give where they are struck; the kick pedal swings; harp strings blur like real strings.
- **Smoother and sharper:** 90 Hz with a quality governor (it steps down to 72 Hz or coarser foveation only if frames
  are late); Fast Motion hand tracking only while you drum; passthrough starts only when you enter "Play in my room";
  the piano remembers where you put it in your room.

### Review fixes (2026-11-01, after the upgrade)

An independent review of the upgrade found these; all are fixed (details: section 21 of `Docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`).

- **The room now matches Meta's direct sound.** Meta's HRTF is about 3-4 dB quieter in the bass and up to 6 dB louder
  around 2 kHz than the head model the room was designed against, and it has no distance delay, so on the headset the
  room was several dB too wet and muddy in the low and middle range. The room unit now scales its reflections and its
  tail per band and per instrument distance to Meta's measured direct sound (`RoomConst.ReferenceDb`, `ReferenceDistDb`)
  and lines its reflections up with Meta's 20-sample latency. At the piano seat the direct-to-room ratio is now the
  design's within 0.3 dB per band (before: 4 dB too wet in the bass, 2 dB too dry in the highs); measured in the editor's
  Meta XR Audio by `PlayMode/Audio/RoomCalibrationTests`.
- **Full quality comes back after a heavy moment.** The audio CPU budget could only go down: one heavy second on the
  headset left the piano's and the harp's detail reduced for the rest of the session. It now climbs back to the default.
- **Large real rooms in "Play in my room":** reflections longer than the delay line are left out instead of piling up
  into an 84 ms slap echo.
- **Hands:** a straight poke at the menu no longer bends the drawn finger sideways (the finger stop fades out near the
  surface normal); harp strings hide fingers behind them again and stay in front of the real hand in mixed reality;
  shadows lie on the drum heads and cymbals as drawn and the drawn stick never sinks into a cymbal; a pressed key's
  shadow is clipped to that key; the menu no longer halves its own hover glow; a nod kick or playback no longer holds
  the head dent and the beater for an extra frame; in mixed reality the finger shadows follow the real (delayed) hand;
  in a dim room Sunny shows "More light helps your hands" once.

Listen to the real Unity output, rendered offline through the integrated engine (new sound, new sound without the room,
and Classic for each instrument) by `EditMode/Audio/FinalDemoTests` into `Builds/Listen/final/` (`*-new.wav`,
`*-new-dry.wav`, `*-classic.wav`, `levels.txt`). The headset's own direction cues (Meta's HRTF) and the Classic room
reverb only run on the device, so these files use a plain stereo pan instead.

## Open it in Unity Hub

1. Install Unity **6000.3.27f1** from Unity Hub with the **Android Build Support** module (it includes OpenJDK and
   the Android SDK and NDK).
2. Unity Hub > Projects > Add > Add project from disk > choose this project's folder (the one with `Assets/` and `Tools/`).
3. Open it, then open `Assets/Scenes/Main.unity`. The scene holds only the player rig and an `App` object: the whole
   room is built in code when you press Play.
4. Press Play with no headset connected: after about half a second the desktop camera takes over (see "Desktop
   controls" below). With Meta Quest Link running, the editor uses the headset instead.

The build target is Android. If Unity asks to switch platform, choose Android.

## Build and install on the Quest

All commands run from the project folder and drive Unity headless (close the project in the editor first: only one
Unity may open a project at a time).

```sh
Tools/unity.sh build      # bakes any missing texture, then builds Builds/Android/VRMusicRoom.apk (about 2.5 min the first time)
Tools/unity.sh install    # adb install -r -g, then starts the app on the headset
Tools/unity.sh log        # live device log (filter one module with: | grep '\[Piano\]')
```

First time on a headset:

1. Turn on developer mode: in the Meta Horizon phone app, Menu > Devices > your headset > Developer Mode (this needs
   a free Meta developer account).
2. Connect the headset to the Mac with a USB-C cable, put it on and accept "Allow USB debugging" (tick "Always allow").
3. `Tools/unity.sh install`. It uses Unity's own `adb`
   (`/Applications/Unity/Hub/Editor/6000.3.27f1/PlaybackEngines/AndroidPlayer/SDK/platform-tools/adb`); any `adb`
   works: `adb install -r -g Builds/Android/VRMusicRoom.apk`.
4. Or use **Meta Quest Developer Hub**: select the headset, then drag `Builds/Android/VRMusicRoom.apk` onto the
   "Apps" panel.
5. On the headset the app is under Library > Unknown Sources > VR Music Room.

When you first choose "Play in my room" the headset asks for permission to use your space (spatial data); without a
Space Setup the menu offers "Set up my room".

room's listener stage 0.11 ms of it, with the Meta reference). The looper with six layers of 240 events a pass costs the
main thread 10 us per frame on average (46 us at most). Alone:
piano 0.06-0.08 ms, drums 0.17-0.19 ms, harp 0.03 ms, room 0.08 ms. The Quest 3S is expected to be 3-4 times slower,
about 1.0-1.4 ms average against the 2 ms budget (to be measured on the headset).

## What must be checked on the headset

The app has run on the user's Quest twice (2026-11-01: hand tracking and the piano work; the fix that lets the palm
open the menu is kept in this version, byte for byte). The upgrade and the merged features have not run on a headset
yet, and none of the new instruments or the music book has. The device checklist is section 19 of
`Docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` (97 items; 91-97 are the integration's: all eight
together, the handpan, the hand drums, the marimba, the bowls and the book, each pointing at its own list;
30-47 are the upgrade's, 48-66 the start panel, Exit, "Move instruments", the grab pinch and the looper's, 67-78 the
first five minutes': the first run's timing and the first note within 10 s, the warm ghost hand and blocks over the room
and passthrough, poke to skip with real hand tracking, the celebration, the drum targets, the tips' moments; 79-87 the
platform's; 88-90 the first five on the platform: the invitation's point and pinch, the soft ghost hand, one scheduler
for every tip). The
important ones:

- **The palm menu** still opens (the hotfix survived the merge), and the hand stays visible while it does.
- **Sound by ear:** the piano (half pedal, re-strikes, the bass), the drums (fingertip strokes, the snare buzz from the
  kick, the crash and its choke), the harp (pokes and sweeps, a palm stopping it), and the room (natural, not boomy;
  "Play in my room" sounds like your room).
- **Audio CPU:** a `-development` build prints `[Audio] dsp avg/p99/max` once a second; with everything playing it must
  stay well under 2 ms per block, with no crackles. Also the per-unit lines (`[Audio] piano ...`, `[Room] ...`).
- **90 Hz:** the `[RIG] quality rung` lines show whether the governor had to step down (to coarser foveation, then
  72 Hz); `adb logcat -s VrApi` FPS over ten minutes with the fire and the snow.
- **Hands:** fingers stop on the keys and heads instead of sinking in; contact shadows visible but not heavy; sticks
  bounce; the skin looks right by the fire. Fast Motion Mode only while drumming: `adb logcat -e FMM`.
- **Mixed reality:** passthrough now starts when you enter "Play in my room" (`[RIG] passthrough ready X s`, at most
  4 s, the room stays up meanwhile); the hands become a clean cut-out; the piano goes back to where you put it last time.
- **Earlier items still open:** the delay from fingertip to ear (`[Audio] latency-probe` lines),
  `AudioModule.PreferredDspBuffer = 128`, the menu text crisp and steady, texture quality, Horizon OS v60 or later,
  no internet permission.
- **Start panel, Exit, "Move instruments"** (items 48-57): the panel readable and pokeable at launch; Exit's double
  poke closes cleanly; near and ray grabs on the real tracker, the heights, the Done / Reset panel's reach; the new
  warm, calm highlight visible enough without looking like glowing UI.
- **The pinch** (items 50, 55, 66): a light pinch is now enough for moving instruments, sticks, the hi-hat, the seat
  turn, the two-hand summon and placing the piano in your room; the palm-toward-your-face system pinch never does any
  of them.
- **The looper** (items 58-65): `Looper.InputLatency` (30 ms, a guess) by ear on an overdub; the 2.8 cm button pitch;
  label legibility; the loop's visuals lead its sound by up to 80 ms; Sunny's perch during GUIDE; the click next to
  the loop; six layers for audio CPU.

## Known issues

- **The new instruments and the music book have never run on a headset.** Their feel (velocity, aim, tracker
  jitter), their sound by ear, the audio CPU on the Quest 3S and the book's readability are open (section 19, 91-97).
- The marimba never shares a set with the drum kit (its air boxes keep the hi-hat away from your right hand): with the
  kit, bringing the marimba plays it alone.
- `InstrumentConformanceTests.TickAllocatesNothing` allocated once each for one instrument and the kit in two of five runs
  and never again; the test now logs which call allocated.

- The upgrade, the start panel, Exit, "Move instruments" and the looper have not run on a headset yet (see the checklist
  above); the Quest 3S audio CPU is an estimate.
- The looper (LOOPER-NOTES section 5): keys, heads and strings animate when a loop note is sent, up to 80 ms before it
  sounds; two layers playing the same piano key share one playback presser (one layer's release lifts both); the harp's
  70 ms re-pluck guard runs on frame time, so two loop plucks of one string closer than ~70 ms (only from two layers)
  may drop the second; `Looper.InputLatency` (30 ms) is a guess to tune by ear.
- The first five minutes (Guide/FIRSTFIVE-NOTES.md) have not run on a headset: Sunny's timings (8 s re-demo, 15 s
  give-up), the poke radius and speed, the ghost hand's alpha over passthrough, the celebration's light levels, and
  whether the first note really comes within 10 s for a new player all need the device. The piano is not fitted to the
  player's height on the first run (the spec's S1); "Move instruments" does it by hand.
- The room's bass reference (`RoomConst.ReferenceDb[0]` -6.3 dB) is hard to fit in the editor's Meta XR Audio: eight runs
  (each with a new noise seed) fitted -4.8 ... -8.4 dB (mean about -6.7), while the committed value kept the 125-250 Hz
  direct-to-room ratio within 0.5 dB of the design; `RoomCalibrationTests` now checks that ratio for band 0.
- The palm-menu summon and the page-turn sweep still read the fingertip-distance pinch (by design: the summon's hotfix is
  kept as it is, and the sweep only needs "not pinching").
- There is no menu row yet for the room amount (five levels, Off to High); the setting exists
  (`musicroom.roomAmount`, default Natural) and the Instruments screen already has six rows.
- The room splits into three bands, so it follows Meta's direct sound per band but not inside one: at the piano seat
  the 2 kHz octave stays about 3.5 dB drier and the 8 kHz octave 2 dB wetter than the design (the bands themselves
  within 0.3 dB). The reference was measured in the editor's Meta XR Audio 85; re-run `RoomCalibrationTests` (it prints
  the lines to paste) if Meta's plugin changes, and check by ear on the headset.
- Fingertips strike the new drums as a stick tip (the spec's "finger" setting measured 11-29 dB quieter and dull); one
  constant, `DrumSpec.FingertipImplement`, changes it.
- Recorded takes do not store the drum's striking part or the harp stroke's direction; playback uses the defaults.
- The menu and Sunny tests still carry a guard for an old audio start-up race that is fixed; it no longer triggers and
  can be removed in a cleanup.
- The build prints two shader warnings from the mixed-reality fallback snow shader (`pow` of a value that is never
  negative) and IL2CPP notes about large TextMeshPro methods; all harmless.
- On the desktop, the top of Sunny's first speech bubble is cut off at the top of the window; on the headset it is fully
  visible.
